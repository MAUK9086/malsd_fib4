# FIB-4 False Negatives in MASLD: Clinical Phenotypes and an Augmented Triage Model

**Mohammad Ahmadullah Khan**. BS Degree Program in Data Science and Applications, Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai, India.

APASL Single Topic Conference 2026 Kumamoto. Oral presentation FP5-2 (Abstract No. 10340), Free Papers 5-1: MASLD/ALD 1, 19 September 2026. Category: MASLD/MAFLD & Alcohol-related Liver Disease.

**Background:** FIB-4, the guideline-recommended first-line MASLD-fibrosis triage tool, excludes advanced (F3-F4) disease but relies on AST, ALT, and platelet count, near-normal in a recognised obesity-driven phenotype (BMI >=30 kg/m<sup>2</sup>, ALT/AST <40 U/L). Its performance for early-to-moderate (F2+) fibrosis remains poorly characterised. Our aim is to identify FIB-4 false negatives (FN) and develop a supplementary primary-care triage tool.

**Methods:** We used NHANES 2017-2020 (N=1,998 MASLD-eligible adults; complete FibroScan elastography; median age 50 years, 54% male). FN were defined as FIB-4 <1.30 despite liver stiffness measurement (LSM) >=8.0 kPa. FN predictors were characterised via gradient-boosted trees (SHAP) across 40 clinical variables; clustering identified distinct phenotypes. A FIB-4 Plus model (FIB-4 + BMI, waist circumference, and Hepatic Steatosis Index) was developed and benchmarked against NAFLD Fibrosis Score (NFS).

**Results:** Among 1,390 (70%) FIB-4 low-risk patients, 16.6% (95% CI 13.5-20.0%) had F2+ fibrosis. FN rates rose with BMI, from 5.1% (<25 kg/m<sup>2</sup>) to 37.7% (>=40 kg/m<sup>2</sup>), and were 2.5-fold higher in diabetics (29.8% vs 11.8%). Waist circumference and BMI, absent from FIB-4, best predicted missed fibrosis (AUROC 0.754, 0.737). Two phenotypes emerged: Severely Obese Metabolic (n=89; BMI 46.1 kg/m<sup>2</sup>, 67% female, LSM 10.5 kPa) and Dyslipidaemic Metabolic (n=131; BMI 35.3 kg/m<sup>2</sup>, 76% male, 44% diabetic, triglycerides 168 mg/dL). FIB-4 Plus outperformed NFS (AUROC 0.837 vs 0.749; DeLong p=0.019; NRI 9.2%).

**Conclusions:** Supplementing FIB-4 with anthropometric and metabolic parameters could reduce missed diagnoses for BMI >=30 or diabetic primary-care patients. Since fibrosis occurs at lower BMI in Asian populations, validation in lean MASLD phenotypes is warranted.
